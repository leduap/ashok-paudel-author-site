Options as a Strategic Investment was first published in 1980. The fifth edition, reviewed here, came out in August 2012 and runs to 1,072 pages. Its author, Lawrence G. McMillan, is a professional trader and the founder of McMillan Analysis Corporation. The book is often called the reference work on its subject.

## The short answer

A reference book, and a very complete one. Each strategy gets its own chapter explaining how it works, when it suits and what to do as the position changes. It is not for beginners: the publisher says it is written for investors who already have some familiarity with the options market. It is long, dense and dated in places, and a book this thorough can make risky positions look more manageable than they are.

## Who it is for

- Readers who already understand calls and puts and want one dependable book to check their thinking against.
- People studying for work in options or derivatives.
- Traders who use a few strategies and want the full treatment of each.

## Who it is not for

- Beginners. Start with something much shorter.
- Readers who want a book to read from cover to cover.
- Anyone looking for a simple route to income. Options can lose money quickly, and some strategies described can lose more than the amount put in.

## Key ideas

The table of contents on the author's site shows how the book is built:

- **Part I: basic properties of stock options.** Definitions, how options are priced and exercised, and how orders work.
- **Part II: call option strategies.** Thirteen chapters, beginning with covered call writing and running through call buying, naked call writing and a series of spreads.
- **Part III: put option strategies.** Put buying, the sale of a put, straddles, synthetic positions, put spreads and long-dated options known as LEAPS.
- **Part IV: additional considerations.** Options with Treasury bills, arbitrage and mathematical applications.
- **Part V: index options and futures.** Including hedging a share portfolio with index products.
- **Part VI: measuring and trading volatility.** Six chapters, followed by a chapter on taxes and a closing chapter titled "The Best Strategy?".

Many strategy chapters include a section on follow-up action: what to do after the trade is on. The publisher highlights the detailed examples, exhibits and checklists. According to the author's site, the fifth edition added a full discussion of volatility futures and options, and updated the examples for weekly options, lower commissions and current option symbols.

## What works

- **It is complete.** Almost any standard strategy has a chapter.
- **It deals with management, not only entry.** The follow-up sections are where many shorter books stop.
- **It is organised for looking things up.** A reader can go straight to the strategy in question.
- **A study guide is sold alongside it,** with reading assignments and quizzes, for those who want to work through it.

## What to be aware of

- **It is not for beginners.** The publisher says so.
- **It is very long.** Few readers will finish it, and it is not designed for that.
- **The edition dates from 2012.** Markets, products and trading costs have moved on, and the tax chapter describes United States rules as they stood then.
- **Options carry real risk.** Uncovered positions in particular can produce losses far larger than the income received. Understanding a strategy on paper is not the same as being able to carry its losses.
- **The author sells related services,** including newsletters and education, through his own company.

## The verdict

The book to own if you are serious about options and already know the basics. Read the chapters on the strategies you use, and keep the rest for when you need it. If you are still deciding whether options belong in your investing at all, the reviews of [A Random Walk Down Wall Street](../2026-09-28-review-a-random-walk-down-wall-street/) and [The Little Book of Common Sense Investing](../2026-09-21-review-the-little-book-of-common-sense-investing/) cover the case for a simpler approach.

## FAQ

### Is Options as a Strategic Investment suitable for beginners?

No. The publisher describes it as written for investors who have some familiarity with the options market.

### Which edition is current?

The fifth edition, published in August 2012. It is the one linked on this page.

### Is there a study guide?

Yes. The author's site sells a separate study guide with reading assignments and quizzes for each chapter.
