Options as a Strategic Investment was first published in 1980 and has been through several editions. It has a place on the desk of many people who trade options seriously, and it is often described as the reference work on the subject.

## What the book covers

The book is organised by strategy. For each one, Lawrence McMillan explains how it is constructed, what it gains or loses as the share price moves, how to choose among the available contracts, and what to do afterwards if the position goes well or badly.

It begins with the basics of calls and puts, then works through covered call writing, buying and selling options outright, spreads, straddles and combinations. Later sections cover index options, longer-dated options and trading based on volatility.

Readers interested in income strategies will find long chapters on covered call writing and on selling puts. McMillan also shows why selling a put and writing a covered call at the same strike behave in much the same way, which is a useful thing to understand before using either.

## What works

- **Follow-up action gets real attention.** Most introductions stop once the trade is opened. This book spends a great deal of space on what to do next, which is where the difficult decisions are.
- **It is comprehensive.** If a strategy exists, it is probably here, with worked examples.
- **It is measured.** McMillan sets out the risks of each approach alongside the rewards.

## What to be aware of

- **It is very long and dense.** At more than a thousand pages it is a reference book. Few people read it from start to finish.
- **It is not for complete beginners.** The opening chapters cover the basics, but the pace assumes a reader who is comfortable with shares and willing to work through numbers.
- **Parts of it show their age.** Markets, products and trading costs have changed since the most recent edition, and some examples reflect earlier conditions.
- **Options carry real risk.** A book this thorough can make complex positions look manageable. Some of the strategies described can lose more than the money put in.

## Who should read it

Readers who already understand the basics and want a dependable reference to check their thinking against. Read the chapters on the strategies you use, and keep the rest for when you need it.

For a much shorter starting point on one strategy, [The Wheel Strategy Guide](https://financea2z.gumroad.com/l/intixr) explains cash-secured puts and covered calls from the first definition.
