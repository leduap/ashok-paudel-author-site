Most books about entrepreneurship assume you will start from nothing. Buy Then Build, published in 2018, argues for a different route: buy a small company that already has customers, staff and cash flow, then grow it. Walker Deibel calls this acquisition entrepreneurship.

## What the book covers

The case for buying is that a start-up's hardest years are spent finding out whether anyone wants the product. An established business has already answered that question.

The book then follows the process in order:

- **Deciding what to look for,** based on your skills and the kind of work you want, before looking at any listing.
- **Searching,** including how business brokers work and how to approach them.
- **Understanding what a business earns.** Small companies are often valued on seller's discretionary earnings, which add back the owner's pay and one-off costs to show what a new owner could take out.
- **Financing.** In the United States, government-backed loans allow buyers to purchase a business with a relatively small share of their own money.
- **Making an offer, checking the business thoroughly, and taking over.**

## What works

- **It makes a neglected option visible.** Many people who want to own a business have never considered buying one.
- **It covers the whole journey,** from the first search to the first months as owner.
- **It starts with the buyer.** The early chapters on matching a business to your own strengths are sensible advice.

## What to be aware of

- **The tone is optimistic.** Buying a business with borrowed money is a serious risk. If the business falters, the debt remains. The book acknowledges this but spends less time on it than the subject deserves.
- **It is specific to the United States,** particularly the chapters on financing.
- **It is an overview.** Valuing a business and checking a seller's figures are skills in themselves, and a buyer will need professional help with both.
- **The author works in this field,** which is worth knowing when weighing how positive the picture is.

## Who should read it

Readers who have thought about owning a business and assumed that meant starting one. Treat it as a first map, not the full instructions.
