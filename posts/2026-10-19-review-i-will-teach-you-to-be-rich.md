The title sounds like a promise from a late-night advert. The book is better than that. I Will Teach You to Be Rich was first published in 2009, and the second edition, reviewed here, came out in May 2019 from Workman. It is a step-by-step programme for getting your accounts, saving and investing set up so they need little attention afterwards.

## The short answer

One of the most practical personal finance books available to an American reader. It tells you what to do, in what order, and gives you words to use when you phone the bank. Its investing advice is simple and mainstream. The banking, credit and account detail applies to the United States, and the jokey, confident voice is a matter of taste.

## Who it is for

- Readers early in their working lives who want a system, not a philosophy.
- People who know they should sort out their money and keep putting it off.
- Anyone who would sooner spend freely on a few things they love than track every coffee.

## Who it is not for

- Readers outside the United States, unless they are happy to translate every practical step.
- Readers who already have their accounts and investing on autopilot.
- People who dislike a brash tone.

## Key ideas

The book is organised as a six-week programme. The chapter titles of the first edition show the order of work, and the second edition keeps the six-week shape:

- **"Optimize Your Credit Cards".** Start with credit and debt. The publisher says the book covers paying off debt and student loans faster.
- **"Beat the Banks".** Set up bank accounts with no fees and better interest.
- **"Get Ready to Invest".** Open the accounts you will invest through.
- **"Conscious Spending".** Decide what you care about, spend on that without guilt, and cut hard elsewhere.
- **"Save While Sleeping".** Automate the flow of money between accounts so the plan runs without willpower. The publisher highlights this as the author's own system.
- **"The Myth of Financial Expertise" and "Investing Isn't Only for Rich People".** A plain case for simple, low-cost investing over expert stock-picking.

Later chapters deal with maintaining the system and with larger decisions under the heading "A Rich Life". The publisher says the second edition has more than 80 new pages, including new tools and material on money and psychology, and that the book includes word-for-word scripts for things like getting a late fee removed.

## What works

- **It gets people to act.** Each week ends with specific tasks, which is rare in a money book.
- **Automation is the right emphasis.** A system that runs by itself beats good intentions.
- **It does not moralise about small pleasures.** The focus is on the few large decisions that matter most.
- **The scripts are practical.** Many readers do not know they can ask.

## What to be aware of

- **It is written for the United States.** Credit scores, account types and the products named are American.
- **The tone divides readers.** It is informal and sure of itself.
- **"Rich" is defined loosely.** The book is about living well on your own terms, not about a number, and the title promises more than any book can.
- **Named products date.** Specific accounts and offers change after publication; the method matters more than the brand.
- **It is light on the hard cases.** Readers with low or irregular income will find less that fits.

## The verdict

A strong first book for an American reader in their twenties or thirties who wants things set up properly and then left alone. Elsewhere, read it for the approach and find the local equivalents yourself. For the thinking behind the investing chapters, see the review of [The Little Book of Common Sense Investing](../2026-09-21-review-the-little-book-of-common-sense-investing/).

## FAQ

### What is new in the second edition?

According to the publisher, more than 80 new pages, including new tools and material on money and psychology.

### Does the six-week programme really take six weeks?

The book sets out one block of tasks per week. How long each takes depends on how many accounts you need to open or change.

### Is it useful outside the United States?

The ideas of automating your money and spending deliberately apply anywhere. The account types, credit system and products do not.
