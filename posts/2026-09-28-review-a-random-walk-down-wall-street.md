A Random Walk Down Wall Street was first published in 1973. Its author, Burton G. Malkiel, is an economist at Princeton University, and he has revised the book many times since. The thirteenth edition came out in 2023 to mark fifty years in print. It remains one of the fullest introductions to investing written for a general reader.

## The short answer

A broad, patient explanation of why share prices are hard to predict and why most investors are better served by saving steadily and holding a diversified set of index funds. It covers far more ground than a short index-fund book, including the history of bubbles and the main ways professionals try to pick shares. It is long, and some chapters are heavier than others.

## Who it is for

- Readers who want to understand the reasoning and evidence behind index investing, not only the conclusion.
- Investors who are tempted by stock tips, charts or star fund managers and want to know what the research says.
- Students of finance who want a readable companion to a textbook.

## Who it is not for

- Readers who want a quick answer. A shorter book will get you to the same place.
- Readers outside the United States looking for local guidance on accounts and tax.
- Anyone hoping for a method to beat the market. The book argues against the idea.

## Key ideas

- **Prices move unpredictably.** The "random walk" of the title is the idea that short-term changes in share prices cannot be reliably forecast, so consistently beating the market average is very hard.
- **Two ways of picking shares, both tested.** Malkiel examines technical analysis, which studies charts of past prices, and fundamental analysis, which studies a company's business and finances, against academic research. He finds significant flaws in both for most investors.
- **Past fund performance is a poor guide.** He cites studies showing that funds which do well in one period often fall behind in the next.
- **The practical conclusion.** According to the publisher, the book shows why an individual who saves consistently and buys a diversified set of index funds can achieve above-average results.
- **New fashions, old patterns.** The publisher says the fiftieth anniversary edition analyses cryptocurrencies, NFTs and meme stocks, and covers tax-aware investing and newer techniques such as factor investing, risk parity and ESG portfolios.

Earlier editions were built around chapters on how assets are valued, the history of speculative manias, behavioural finance, and a guide to investing at different stages of life. This review has not compared the thirteenth edition's chapter list with those.

## What works

- **It teaches how to think about markets.** A reader finishes able to weigh a new investment idea, not only knowing one recommended product.
- **It takes the other side seriously.** Stock-picking methods are explained fairly before they are tested.
- **It is kept up to date.** Few investing books have been revised as often.
- **The writing is clear** for a book by an academic economist.

## What to be aware of

- **It is long.** The paperback of the thirteenth edition runs to more than 400 pages.
- **The middle is denser than the rest.** The sections on portfolio theory ask more of the reader than the history or the practical advice.
- **It is written for American investors.** The practical guidance assumes United States accounts and tax rules.
- **Not everyone accepts the theory in full.** How efficient markets are is still debated among economists. The book's practical advice does not depend on markets being perfectly efficient, only on their being hard to beat after costs.

## The verdict

The best single book for a reader who wants to understand why the case for index funds is made, and who is willing to give it the time. ## FAQ

### Which edition is the latest?

The thirteenth edition, published in 2023 for the book's fiftieth anniversary. The paperback is the one linked on this page.

### What does "random walk" mean?

That the next move in a share price cannot be predicted from its past moves. It is the reason the book doubts that anyone can pick winners consistently.

### Do I need the newest edition?

The core argument has been the same for decades. The newer editions add discussion of recent developments such as cryptocurrencies and meme stocks.
