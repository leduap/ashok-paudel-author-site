John C. Bogle founded Vanguard and launched the first index fund available to ordinary investors. This book, first published in 2007 and updated for a tenth-anniversary edition, is his case for that idea in its simplest form.

## What the book covers

Bogle's argument rests on arithmetic that is difficult to dispute. All investors together own the market, so together they earn the market's return. Take away the costs of fund management, trading and advice, and investors as a group must earn less than the market. The more they pay, the further behind they fall.

His conclusion is that most people should own the entire market through a low-cost index fund and hold it for a very long time. His image for this is that instead of searching for the needle, you buy the haystack.

The book walks through the supporting evidence: how few actively managed funds stay ahead over long periods, how past winners tend to fall back towards the average, and how investors hurt their own returns by moving money in and out at the wrong times.

## What works

- **The logic is simple and durable.** The core argument does not depend on any forecast.
- **It puts costs at the centre.** Many readers come away seeing for the first time how much a small annual fee takes over several decades.
- **It quotes other voices.** Each chapter ends with supporting remarks from well-known investors and academics, which shows the idea is not Bogle's alone.

## What to be aware of

- **It is repetitive.** The book makes one point from many angles. Some readers will be persuaded by chapter five and can skim after that.
- **The author is not neutral.** Bogle built a company on this idea. The arithmetic stands on its own, but it is fair to know who is making the case.
- **It is focused on the United States,** in both its data and its fund examples.
- **The data ages.** The figures stop at the edition's date. The argument has held up since, but check newer numbers if you want them.

## Who should read it

Anyone who invests through funds and has not thought hard about what they pay. It is also a good book to hand to someone who believes that picking the right fund manager is the main task.
