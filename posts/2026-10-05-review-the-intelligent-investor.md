The Intelligent Investor was first published in 1949 and last revised by Benjamin Graham in the early 1970s. Warren Buffett, who studied under Graham, has called it the best book on investing ever written. Modern editions add commentary by the journalist Jason Zweig after each chapter.

## What the book covers

Graham begins by separating investment from speculation. An investment, in his terms, is made after thorough analysis and promises safety of the money put in and an adequate return. Anything else is speculation.

He then describes two kinds of investor. The **defensive** investor wants to avoid serious mistakes with little effort, and should hold a simple, diversified mix of shares and bonds. The **enterprising** investor is willing to do substantial work in search of better results, and Graham is frank that this is harder than it looks.

Two ideas from the book have outlived everything else in it:

- **Mr. Market.** Graham asks you to imagine a business partner who offers every day to buy your share or sell you his, at prices that swing with his mood. You are free to ignore him. The point is that market prices are offers, not instructions.
- **Margin of safety.** Pay sufficiently less than your estimate of what something is worth, so that you can be wrong and still come out all right.

## What works

- **Those two ideas are worth the whole book.** They shape how many careful investors think.
- **It is about temperament as much as technique.** Graham argues that an investor's main problem is likely to be themselves.
- **Zweig's commentary helps.** It updates the examples and explains what has changed.

## What to be aware of

- **It is dated.** Many chapters discuss securities, companies and market conditions from decades ago. The detailed selection rules were written for a different world.
- **It is not an easy read.** The prose is formal and the middle chapters are slow.
- **Most readers are defensive investors.** Graham's own advice for them is simpler than the reputation of the book suggests.

## Who should read it

Readers who want to understand where value investing comes from. If you want only the essentials, read the chapter on market fluctuations and the chapter on margin of safety, which are chapters 8 and 20 in the revised edition.
