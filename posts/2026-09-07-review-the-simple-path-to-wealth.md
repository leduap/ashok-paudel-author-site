The Simple Path to Wealth grew out of a series of letters JL Collins wrote to his daughter and later published on his blog. That origin shows. The book reads like advice from a relative who has made the mistakes already and wants to save you the trouble.

## What the book covers

The argument fits in one sentence: avoid debt, spend less than you earn, and invest the surplus in a low-cost fund that owns the whole stock market. The rest of the book explains why each part matters and what to do at different stages of life.

Collins splits an investor's life into two phases. In the wealth-building phase, you are working and adding money, so falling markets are a chance to buy more. In the wealth-preservation phase, you are living off the portfolio, so he adds bonds to smooth the ride. He also covers how much a portfolio can support in retirement, built around the well-known 4% guideline.

## What works

- **It is short on products and long on behaviour.** The book spends more time on staying calm during a crash than on choosing funds, which is where most investors actually go wrong.
- **The idea of financial independence as freedom.** Collins frames savings as the ability to say no, which makes the case for saving more persuasive than a spreadsheet does.
- **Chapters are short and stand alone.** You can read one in ten minutes.

## What to be aware of

- **It is written for readers in the United States.** The chapters on account types, and the specific funds named, do not transfer directly to other countries. The principles do; the details need local translation.
- **The portfolio is concentrated in one country's stock market.** Collins explains his reasons, but many readers and many other authors prefer a global fund. It is worth reading a second view on this point.
- **It repeats itself.** That is partly deliberate, since the chapters began as separate blog posts.

## Who should read it

Readers who are new to investing and want one clear plan will get the most from it. Experienced investors will find little that is new, but may still value the chapters on behaviour.
