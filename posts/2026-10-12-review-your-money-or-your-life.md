Your Money or Your Life was first published in 1992 and grew out of seminars given by Joe Dominguez. He died in 1997. Vicki Robin revised the book for a new edition in 2018, with a foreword by the blogger Mr. Money Mustache, and that is the edition reviewed here. It is one of the books behind the modern financial independence movement, and it starts from a different place than most money books: not how to earn more, but what your earning costs you.

## The short answer

A programme for changing how you think about spending, built on one idea: money is something you trade your life's hours for. The nine steps ask for honest, detailed record-keeping over months. Readers who do the work often find it changes what they buy. The investing guidance is brief, and the tone is closer to a way of life than to a finance manual.

## Who it is for

- People who earn enough and still feel short, and want to know where it goes.
- Readers drawn to financial independence who want its original text.
- Anyone who wants to connect spending with what they value.

## Who it is not for

- Readers who want a quick fix. The programme takes months of tracking.
- Readers looking mainly for investing detail.
- People who dislike a reflective, values-led tone.

## Key ideas

The nine steps, as the author's own summary names them:

1. **Making peace with the past.** Add up everything you have ever earned, then draw up what you have to show for it.
2. **Tracking your life energy.** Work out your real hourly wage by counting the time and money your job costs you, such as commuting and work clothes. Then keep track of every amount that comes in or goes out.
3. **The monthly tabulation.** Sort a month's spending into categories and convert each into hours of life.
4. **Three questions.** For each category, ask whether it brought fulfilment in proportion to the hours spent, and whether it fits your values and purpose.
5. **Making life energy visible.** Plot monthly income and spending on a wall chart and put it where you will see it.
6. **Minimising spending,** by valuing your life energy.
7. **Maximising income,** on the same principle.
8. **Capital and the crossover point.** Track the income your savings could produce. The crossover point is when that line rises above your monthly expenses.
9. **Managing your finances.** Learn enough about long-term investing to look after your capital.

The publisher says the 2018 edition adds modern topics, including investing in index funds, side income and freelancing, tracking finances online, and talking about money with others.

## What works

- **The central idea changes behaviour.** Pricing a purchase in hours of work is simple and hard to forget.
- **It deals with spending without preaching a budget.** The publisher describes it as saving through mindfulness and good habits, not strict budgeting.
- **The steps build on each other,** and the wall chart gives quick feedback.
- **The revision brought it up to date.** The original programme pointed readers to long-term government bonds; the current edition covers index funds.

## What to be aware of

- **The record-keeping is heavy.** Tracking every amount, every month, is more than many readers will keep up.
- **Some steps assume United States records.** The summary itself notes that earnings history may be hard to obtain elsewhere.
- **The investing step is the thinnest.** It is a starting point, not a guide.
- **The tone is earnest.** The book cares about consumption and the environment as much as about money, which some readers will welcome and others will not.
- **Living on investment income is not a guarantee.** The crossover point depends on returns that vary, and on expenses staying where you expect.

## The verdict

Worth reading for steps two to four alone. Even a reader who never draws the wall chart is likely to look at spending differently afterwards. For the investing side, pair it with one of the index-fund books reviewed here, such as [The Simple Path to Wealth](../2026-09-07-review-the-simple-path-to-wealth/).

## FAQ

### What are the nine steps?

Making peace with the past, tracking your life energy, the monthly tabulation, three questions, making life energy visible, minimising spending, maximising income, capital and the crossover point, and managing your finances.

### What is the crossover point?

The month when the income your savings could produce is higher than your expenses. In the book it marks financial independence.

### Is the 2018 edition very different from the original?

The nine steps are the same. The publisher says the revision adds index funds, side income, online tracking and conversations about money.
