Your Money or Your Life was first published in 1992 and has been revised since. It is one of the books behind the modern financial independence movement, and it starts from a different place than most money books: not how to earn more, but what your earning costs you.

## What the book covers

The book is a nine-step programme. Its central idea is that money is something you trade your **life energy** for. You have a limited number of hours, and every purchase is paid for with some of them.

Several of the steps follow from that:

- **Work out your real hourly wage.** Take what you earn and subtract the costs of having the job, such as commuting and work clothes. Add the unpaid hours the job consumes. The result is usually lower than the figure on your contract.
- **Track every bit of money that comes in or goes out.**
- **Each month, ask of every category of spending** whether it gave you fulfilment in proportion to the hours it cost, and whether it fits your values.
- **Chart your income and expenses over time.** The book's goal is the crossover point, where income from investments exceeds monthly expenses.

## What works

- **The life-energy idea changes how purchases feel.** Translating a price into hours of work is simple and stays with you.
- **It does not prescribe a budget.** The book asks questions and lets you decide what is worth it, which makes it less preachy than its reputation.
- **It connects money to purpose.** The aim is enough, not more.

## What to be aware of

- **The tracking is demanding.** Recording everything, indefinitely, is more than many readers will sustain. A few months is enough to learn most of what the exercise teaches.
- **The investing section is the weakest part.** The original edition favoured government bonds, at a time when they paid far more. Later editions were updated, but other books cover investing better.
- **The tone will not suit everyone.** Parts of it read as a philosophy of life as much as a finance guide.

## Who should read it

Readers who earn a reasonable income and still feel they are not getting anywhere. It is a better book about spending than about investing, and it works well alongside a practical guide to index funds.
